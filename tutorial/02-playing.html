2. Playing and octaves

Keys, octave, transpose, mono and poly

On this page
  • The keys
  • Octave shift
  • Transpose
  • Volume

The keys

Switch on and press any key: you hear the loaded voice (the first one is 001 PIANO 1). The 27 keys are not velocity sensitive: they are simple switches, so every note has the same loudness (see Velocity).

The keyboard has two rows. The lower row has 16 “white” keys, the upper row 11 “black” keys, arranged like a piano in groups of 3-2-3-2-1. The lowest key is F and the highest G; at octave 0 the range is about F3 to G5.

Each key has a thin LED stripe that lights while you hold it.

The keys change meaning with the mode
  • Normal / Home: they play notes.
  • ARP on: held keys feed the arpeggiator.
  • SEQ on: notes you play are recorded into steps, and the 16 white keys light up as step indicators.
  • EDIT on: the black keys become OP1–OP6, PIT, GLO, MONO and POLY selectors. Notes are not played.

Octave shift

  1. Press OCT+ to move the whole keyboard up one octave, or OCT− to move it down. The range is −3 to +3 octaves.

    The screen briefly shows Octave and the value.

  2. Read the LED next to the button:

    OctaveLED
    0both off
    ±1slow blink
    ±2fast blink
    ±3solid

    If the lit LED is on the OCT− side, the octave is negative.

  3. Press OCT− and OCT+ together to reset the octave and the transpose to 0.

Transpose (up to ±12 semitones)

  1. Hold OCT+ (to go up) or OCT− (to go down).

  2. While holding it, turn ALGORITHM. The screen shows Transpose and the value, between −12 and +12 semitones.

  3. Release the button. Transpose is stored in the global settings automatically and survives a restart.

Two different “transposes”

This front-panel transpose (±12, global) is not the same as Transpose on the voice’s EDIT → GLO page (0–48, part of each voice, 24 = no shift) or the sequencer’s per-pattern Transpose (±24). They add up.

For the curious: the MIDI note number for a key is its position (0–26) + 53 + octave × 12 + transpose, so the lowest key at octave 0 is note 53 (F3).

Volume

Turn MASTER. It controls the final output of everything including effects. If a voice sounds quiet, check MASTER, then the OutLevel of the operators (page 5).

Mono and poly

Normally the FM-1 is polyphonic: up to 12 notes at once. In EDIT mode you can switch to MONO, where only one note plays at a time (the last one you pressed wins). Mono is for basses and leads, and it is the only mode where Glide works (page 9).

  1. Press EDIT.

  2. Press the black key labelled MONO or POLY. The active one’s LED is solid, the other one blinks.

  3. Press EDIT again to leave.

Is mono/poly saved with the voice?

The manual does not say whether the mono/poly choice is stored per voice or globally. Check by switching voices on your unit.

Velocity

The keys cannot sense how hard you press. The firmware has a Velocity setting on the GLO page (1–127) that fixes the loudness of every key press; incoming MIDI notes keep the velocity they carry. Operators can respond to velocity: that is the Key Velo setting on each operator’s Sens page.
